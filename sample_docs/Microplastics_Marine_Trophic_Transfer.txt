FIELD RESEARCH STUDY: Micro- and Nanoplastic Accumulation in Coastal Estuaries
Department of Marine Ecology & Environmental Toxicology
Principal Investigator: Dr. Henrik Lindqvist
Published: July 2025

1. Introduction & Sampling Methodology
Water column, sediment, and biological tissue samples were collected across 18 stations in the North Atlantic estuarine zone over a 14-month monitoring campaign. Microplastics (1 µm to 5 mm) and nanoplastics (<1 µm) were identified via micro-FTIR and Raman spectroscopy.

2. Trophic Transfer Quantification
Evidence of trophic biomagnification was observed across primary producers (phytoplankton), primary consumers (calanoid copepods), and secondary consumers (juvenile herring and flounder). Mean particle loads increased from 4.2 particles/individual in zooplankton to 38.6 particles/g wet weight in secondary teleost fish liver tissues, demonstrating a trophic transfer factor of 2.14.

3. Chemical Leachate Bioavailability & Endocrine Disruption
High concentrations of phthalate plasticizers (DEHP, DBP) and antioxidant additives (BHT) were detected in digestive gland fluids. Histological examination revealed localized gut epithelial inflammation and cellular vacuolation in 41% of examined specimens.

4. Open Scientific Debates & Uncertainty
While laboratory exposure studies using pristine spherical polystyrene beads report high cellular mortality, natural environmental particles are weathered and colonized by microbial biofilms ('plastisphere'), altering cellular uptake kinetics. Reconciling laboratory toxicity thresholds with wild environmental exposure concentrations remains a critical research frontier.
